Invented planet No. 009: Thessaly. A planet for people who check the weather app ten times a day.

Rosa is in the first frame, before anyone mentions her.

The rain on Thessaly lifts off the sea in thin threads and gathers in shallow pools on the undersides of the clouds, which turn gold at dusk. Every pool has a name, and the argument about which is deepest is three hundred years old. Umbrellas are worn feet-first from October to March, and boots are waterproof on top. People now date things 'before the Down' and 'after the Down'.

On my first day I held my umbrella over my head too. Rosa was the only one who didn't laugh. She has not said 'I told you so'. She's had it embroidered on a cushion.

The forecast now reads: from below. Probably.

300,000 people, a 23-hour day, 0.6 g. The rain rides a steady updraft off the warm sea. (On Earth, an updraft of about 90 km/h can hold a golf-ball-sized hailstone in the air.) That afternoon, Thessaly's dropped for most of an hour.
