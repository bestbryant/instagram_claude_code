Invented planet No. 019b: Tessel. A planet for the double-checker.

Count the days on Jess's board.

This is part two of Tessel. In part one, Jess lost count for the first time in her life, and the village kept the board going without her.

Tessel is 90,000 people on a slow, tilted world: winter lasts a year, and summer about a fortnight. With no sun to count by, every village has someone who keeps the date. Jess has kept hers since she was a girl, and her wages are the first slice of every birthday cake in the village.

When she got her chalk back, she checked the village's sums, the way you'd mark homework. Day 213 was on the board twice. It was her birthday. The village had wanted more cake. Jess has never let a sum go in her life. She found no mistakes.

I went to both. I'd only brought one card.

(King Charles has two birthdays: his real one in November, and an official one in June, with a parade. The story goes that George II started it in 1748, because his own birthday was too cold for marching. On Tessel there's no warmer month to move it to, so the village just had it twice.)

Is there a day you'd count twice?
