# Accounts to follow and talk to

Twenty real, active illustration and worldbuilding accounts close to the Atlas look or humour, checked on 19 Oct 2026 (handle exists, belongs to the artist, posting in 2025-26). For the owner to follow and engage with **by hand**. The API can't comment on other accounts, and automated comments get new accounts flagged.

How: follow all of them once. Then 2-3 genuine comments a day on a recent post. Never mention Atlas, never ask for a follow. The comment ideas are starting points: check the post exists, and put it in your own words.

## Space & visual jokes

**[@tomgauld](https://www.instagram.com/tomgauld/)** · Tom Gauld · ~177K  
Makes deadpan one-panel cartoons about books, science and space, with tiny flat figures and a printed look. He's the closest match to Atlas's one-dry-joke-per-planet tone, and his followers read sci-fi and like illustration.  
*Comment idea:* On his June 2026 New Yorker cover (the backpacker's selfie in front of the old-master landscape): "Spare a thought for the tiny rider on the cliff top. He's been posing up there for three hundred years and the selfie still crops him out."

**[@scottlistfield](https://www.instagram.com/scottlistfield/)** · Scott Listfield · 75K  
Paints a lone astronaut wandering a strange near-future Earth, with a deadpan pop-culture joke in each painting. Space plus one dry gag per image is Atlas's formula, and at 75K followers he's likely to read comments.  
*Comment idea:* On 'Blue Bird' (the astronaut facing a crumbling Twitter-bird statue): "The astronaut squaring up to a crumbling Twitter bird like it's Ozymandias. Look on my posts, ye mighty."

**[@mattiasink](https://www.instagram.com/mattiasink/)** · Mattias Adolfsson · ~221K  
Fills ink-and-watercolour sketchbooks with tiny people, odd machines and invented buildings. He uses the same tiny-figures-doing-absurd-things humour as Atlas, inside invented places.  
*Comment idea:* On the sketchbook 45 opening spread (the room that flips upside down): "Gravity clearly gave up halfway up that room, and the elephant in the doorway is the only one taking it calmly."

**[@stevethomasart](https://www.instagram.com/stevethomasart/)** · Steve Thomas · ~5.5K  
Makes mid-century-style travel posters, including Space Travel and Fantasy Travel series with posters for Europa and Triton. These are travel posters for other worlds, and his account is small enough that he'll notice comments.  
*Comment idea:* On the Live Science fusion-engine illustration (Oct 14): "Somehow you made a fusion engine look like an ocean liner from 1955. I'd book a ticket to Triton off this alone."

**[@icreature](https://www.instagram.com/icreature/)** · Invisible Creature (Don and Ryan Clark) · 54K  
Seattle studio behind NASA/JPL's Visions of the Future planet travel posters, with a screen-print misregistration look. They defined the retro planet-poster style, and their followers are design and space-poster fans.  
*Comment idea:* On the Height Fight Kickstarter post: "I need to know how Machu Picchu gets measured against a Ferris wheel. This is going to start arguments at dinner."

**[@emilianoponzi](https://www.instagram.com/emilianoponzi/)** · Emiliano Ponzi · 81K  
Italian editorial illustrator who makes flat-colour conceptual images with tiny figures in vast spaces and one idea per image. That's the same single-idea, small-silhouette approach Atlas uses.  
*Comment idea:* On 'Breath', the hilltop flower sculpture in Peccioli: "A flower that opens at dawn and closes at sunset is such a calm idea. Does it ever hesitate halfway on a cloudy morning?"

**[@peltier.r](https://www.instagram.com/peltier.r/)** · Ryan Peltier · ~3.1K  
Minneapolis editorial illustrator who sets tiny figures against huge, minimal structures, including in the space comic The Europa Mission. He plays with scale the way Atlas does and is small enough to read every comment.  
*Comment idea:* On a page from The Europa Mission: "The tiny suited figures against that enormous ice wall make Europa feel properly huge. Is the comic going to show what's under the ice?"

## Printed-look landscapes

**[@tomhaugomat](https://www.instagram.com/tomhaugomat/)** · Tom Haugomat · ~265K  
Draws faceless tiny figures in huge, flat, layered landscapes with limited palettes. His visual style is almost the same as Atlas's, and his large audience likes quiet, contemplative landscapes.  
*Comment idea:* On the SPHERE autumn 2025 cover: "The migrating birds are the only things moving in the whole scene, and somehow that makes the room feel even quieter. 'A state of mind' is exactly it."

**[@ryotakemasa](https://www.instagram.com/ryotakemasa/)** · Ryo Takemasa · ~79K  
Makes grainy landscapes and quiet scenes with small figures, using only a few ink colours (clients include NYT and Hermes). His grain and two- or three-colour restraint are the closest thing to Atlas's screen-print look.  
*Comment idea:* On 'The Hill of Red Kochia' (posted Oct 16): "Those kochia look like they'd be soft if you patted them. Is the red a single ink with the grain doing all the shading?"

**[@mcmintea](https://www.instagram.com/mcmintea/)** · Maggie Chiang · 81K  
Paints dreamy layered landscapes with tiny wanderers in teal and peach. She shares Atlas's tiny-figure-in-a-big-world mood and its flat stacked planes. Her handle is @mcmintea, not @maggiechiang.  
*Comment idea:* On her newest piece (the pink figure gliding between floating teal lily pads): "Commuting by floating lily pad is the dream. The little gold sparkles against that grainy cream-to-teal sky are perfect."

**[@jonmcn](https://www.instagram.com/jonmcn/)** · Jon McNaught · ~28K  
Makes quiet, slow comics and London Review of Books covers in screen-print-like limited palettes with small figures. His dry, observational printed-look work matches Atlas's nine-minute-night humour.  
*Comment idea:* On the gull linocut (Aug 2025; use his newest print if there's a more recent one): "That two-blue roller blend on the gull is so smooth. Are both inks on the roller at once, or is it two passes?"

**[@laura_boswell_printmaker](https://www.instagram.com/laura_boswell_printmaker/)** · Laura Boswell · ~65K  
Makes Japanese-style woodblock and linocut landscapes with limited palettes, and posts process videos. She was voted Printmaker of the Year at the 2025 Festival of Print. Her work is flat, layered and printed, and her audience is curious about process.  
*Comment idea:* On 'Three Ravens': "The ravens as tiny specks are doing all the work. Those mountains only feel huge because of them. Are the two terracotta ridges separate blocks?"

**[@haxloeffler](https://www.instagram.com/haxloeffler/)** · Max Loffler · ~61K  
German gigposter artist whose screen prints use bold limited colours and retro pop-art humour (Clio Music 2026 gold). His followers are screen-print collectors who care about ink layers and grain, the look Atlas imitates. The handle starts with 'hax'.  
*Comment idea:* On the Black Keys Laval poster: "A reaper lugging a giant peach, done up like a vintage fruit-can label, is wonderfully weird. How many colours did the run take?"

## Maps & worldbuilding

**[@simon_stalenhag](https://www.instagram.com/simon_stalenhag/)** · Simon Stalenhag · 211K  
Paints narrative sci-fi landscapes (Tales from the Loop, The Electric State) where small lone figures stand inside a whole alternate world. His followers love seeing a whole world in one image.  
*Comment idea:* On the Sunset at Zero Point plates: "Putting a map of the island in with the paintings makes the off-limits lab feel like a real place you could get lost trying to reach. Did the map come before the paintings or after?"

**[@jamesgurneyart](https://www.instagram.com/jamesgurneyart/)** · James Gurney · ~309K  
Created Dinotopia and posts his worldbuilding process and plein-air painting. He's the best-known builder of an invented world people want to visit, and his audience follows worldbuilding art closely.  
*Comment idea:* On the Thermala maquette post (Oct 17): "Burlap, plaster and modelling clay for an alpine stronghold is wonderful. Do you light the maquette to find the painting, or paint a rough first and build the model to check it?"

**[@andrewdegraff](https://www.instagram.com/andrewdegraff/)** · Andrew DeGraff · ~5K  
Draws illustrated maps of journeys from novels and films (Plotted: A Literary Atlas, Cinemaps). It's literally an atlas of fictional places, and his account is small enough that comments get noticed.  
*Comment idea:* On the La La Land 10th-anniversary map: "Does the 'what if' epilogue get its own route on the map? It feels like it should be a dotted line."

**[@francesca.baerald](https://www.instagram.com/francesca.baerald/)** · Francesca Baerald · ~16K  
Hand-draws fantasy world maps for novels, games and RPGs. It's a core fantasy-cartography account, and her followers are the map fans most likely to enjoy an atlas of invented planets.  
*Comment idea:* On the Alchemised Deluxe Edition post (Oct 16) or the UNBOUND wrap-up: "All six UNBOUND stretch goals, congratulations! Will the Camelot map from the signed print be in the book too?"

**[@waynebarlowe_thedarkness](https://www.instagram.com/waynebarlowe_thedarkness/)** · Wayne Barlowe · unknown  
Wrote and painted Expedition, the illustrated field guide to the alien planet Darwin IV, which is now the ORBITSTAR board game. It's the classic tour of an invented planet. The handle is his even though the name sounds unrelated, and his feed leans dark, so pick a post that suits the tone.  
*Comment idea:* On an ORBITSTAR post: "Darwin IV as a board game is a lovely surprise after all these years. Did you draw any new creatures for ORBITSTAR, or are they all from the original Expedition?"

**[@deven_rue](https://www.instagram.com/deven_rue/)** · Deven Rue · 22K  
Professional fantasy cartographer (Critical Role's Tal'Dorei, Magic: The Gathering lands) who also writes the in-world magazine Fantastical Geographic. That 'geographic' framing is close to the Atlas premise, and her TTRPG audience is community-minded.  
*Comment idea:* On 'The Divine Empire of Cirrhul' (Oct 15): "The compass rose process shots are my favourite part. Roughly how long does a rose like that take compared with the coastline?"

**[@danielsmaps](https://www.instagram.com/danielsmaps/)** · Daniel Hasenbos · ~63K  
Hand-drawn fantasy maps for RPGs and novels by Daniel and Laura, released weekly. He's a working cartographer who replies to his community of map and worldbuilding fans.  
*Comment idea:* On 'The Japanese Merchant City of Yurai' (Oct 6): "A town where merchants rival samurai in wealth is a great hook. Did that shape the layout, like the warehouses getting better walls than the castle?"
